What the timing means — F.S. §489.115

Florida requires proof of general liability and workers' compensation (or exemption) before it issues a construction license, so a new record is a timely reason to call. The file tells you the account, the trade, the county, the issue date and — for qualified-business records where DBPR's extract allows it — the qualifying individual and their license number. It does not tell you what they bought, from whom, or when it renews.

How it works

  1. DBPR publishes its construction licensee and applicant files under Chapter 119. We pull them every Tuesday morning.
  2. Licenses dated in the last seven days are pulled, every record is classified (company license, individual qualifying a business, individual without a business), and the applicant register is compared with last week's snapshot so only genuinely new applications appear.
  3. Before anything is sent, the run must pass automated checks — file size, dates, duplicate and match anomalies, region totals — or it does not go out.
  4. You receive one email grouped by territory and county, plus the CSV for your CRM.

Questions agencies ask

Where does the data come from?
Directly from the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation's public-records extracts, refreshed weekly. Nothing is scraped from people-search sites or social networks.
Are phone numbers included?
Not in the current edition. DBPR's applicant register contains phone numbers, but we do not distribute them until the applicability of Florida's Telephone Solicitation Act (F.S. 501.059) to this product is resolved. Every record carries the business name, license type, city and county. Individual certifications also carry the license number; DBPR does not publish one for the qualified-business rows in its bulk extract, so those companies are looked up by name.
Aren't they already insured if the license was issued?
Florida requires proof of coverage before issuance, so most are. What the file gives you is timing and a name; the rewrite, limits and workers' comp conversation is yours to have. A qualified-business record is the license DBPR issues to a business that contracts under a qualifying licensee — the file does not say whether the entity itself is new. Some licensees are qualifying an existing company; the business name column makes that obvious.
How do I update my card, change territory, or cancel?
Card updates and cancellation are self-service in the Stripe customer portal — the link is at the bottom of every report. Cancellations take effect at the end of the period you have paid for and are not pro-rated. To add or change a territory, reply to any report or email support@licensedigest.com; a person confirms the new monthly total before anything changes.
Can't I download this from DBPR myself?
Yes. The raw files are public — a 45 MB licensee file and a 104,000-row cumulative applicant register with no dates. What you are paying for is the weekly comparison, the classification, the territory grouping, the automated quality checks and a report that is normally in your inbox by 9:00 a.m. Eastern on Tuesday. If you would rather do that yourself, you should.
